FRAMEWORK CORPUS  ·  TWELVE IDENTITIES

The first seven frameworks describe how the economy works: credit cycles short and long, deleveragings, paradigm shifts, the changing world order, inflation and currency. The remaining five describe how to invest within: a four-step decision template, the All-Weather portfolio, alpha and portable alpha, risk parity with leverage, and stress-testing for tails.

Each framework rendered with its decision question, mechanism, historical archetype, and worked-example formula — verbatim from Dalio's writings, byte-exact reconciled.

§ 1.1   HOW THE ECONOMIC MACHINE WORKS
STAGE 01 / 04  ·  THE QUESTION

"An economy is simply the sum of the transactions that make it up." Dalio · How the Economic Machine Works · Bridgewater 2012 · p.1
§ 1.1   HOW THE ECONOMIC MACHINE WORKS
STAGE 02 / 04  ·  THE MECHANISM

01
Productivity Long-run trend growth from output-per-hour gains. Slow, smooth, decade-scale.
02
Short-term cycle 5–8 year credit waves driven by central-bank policy. Boom-bust around the productivity trend.
03
Long-term cycle 50–75 year debt accumulation cycles culminating in deleveraging.
§ 1.1   HOW THE ECONOMIC MACHINE WORKS
STAGE 03 / 04  ·  HISTORY

Productivity has trended near 2% real for a century. Short-cycles oscillate ±5pp around it. Long-cycles emerge at debt peaks: 1929–1932, 2008–2009, 2020+.

§ 1.1   HOW THE ECONOMIC MACHINE WORKS
STAGE 04 / 04  ·  THE FORMULA

$$Y_t \;=\; P_t \cdot (1 + g_{ST,t}) \cdot (1 + g_{LT,t})$$
Regime
Productivity sets the trend; cycles are deviations.
§ 1.2   THE SHORT-TERM DEBT CYCLE
STAGE 01 / 04  ·  THE QUESTION

"a shorter-term (typically 5 to 8 years) debt cycle." Dalio · How the Economic Machine Works · Bridgewater 2012 · p.5
§ 1.2   THE SHORT-TERM DEBT CYCLE
STAGE 02 / 04  ·  THE MECHANISM

01
Expansion Low rates → credit growth → spending → income → repeat.
02
Inflation pickup Capacity tightens → wage and price pressure.
03
Tightening Central bank raises rates to cool inflation.
04
Recession Spending contracts, debts go bad, unemployment rises.
05
Easing Central bank cuts rates → cycle restarts.
§ 1.2   THE SHORT-TERM DEBT CYCLE
STAGE 03 / 04  ·  HISTORY

Cycle length 5–8 years, driven by Fed Funds rate cycles. Mild deviations around 2% real productivity trend.

§ 1.2   THE SHORT-TERM DEBT CYCLE
STAGE 04 / 04  ·  THE FORMULA

$$S_t \;=\; I_t + \Delta D_t$$
Regime
Spending equals income plus credit growth.
§ 1.3   THE LONG-TERM DEBT CYCLE
STAGE 01 / 04  ·  THE QUESTION

"big, long-term debt cycles typically last about one lifetime—roughly 80 years." Dalio · How Countries Go Broke Part 1 · Introduction
§ 1.3   THE LONG-TERM DEBT CYCLE
STAGE 02 / 04  ·  THE MECHANISM

01
Accumulation Debt-to-income rises over decades; living standards rise faster than productivity.
02
Bubble Asset prices, debt, and spending peak together; speculation dominates.
03
Deleveraging Debt service can no longer be paid; defaults, restructurings, money printing.
§ 1.3   THE LONG-TERM DEBT CYCLE
STAGE 03 / 04  ·  HISTORY

Each ended a 50–75 year accumulation cycle. Resolution mechanism is the four levers (see §1.4).

§ 1.3   THE LONG-TERM DEBT CYCLE
STAGE 04 / 04  ·  THE FORMULA

$$\text{Debt Service}_t \;=\; D_t \cdot r_t$$
Regime
Debt service growth must not exceed income growth indefinitely.
§ 1.4   DELEVERAGINGS
STAGE 01 / 04  ·  THE QUESTION

"Each one of these four paths reduces debt/income ratios." Dalio · An In-Depth Look at Deleveragings · Bridgewater 2012
§ 1.4   DELEVERAGINGS
STAGE 02 / 04  ·  THE MECHANISM

01
Austerity Cut spending. Deflationary. Reduces debt-service capacity.
02
Defaults Write down debts. Deflationary. Wealth destruction.
03
Money printing Expand monetary base. Inflationary. Debases currency.
04
Redistribution Transfer wealth from creditors to debtors. Politically unstable.
§ 1.4   DELEVERAGINGS
STAGE 03 / 04  ·  HISTORY

US 1930–32 (ugly deflationary); US 1933–37 (beautiful — printing dominant); Japan 1990+ (ugly deflationary lite); Weimar 1921–23 (ugly inflationary).

research/04_deleveragings.md §7 — lever-mix percentages (DERIVED from narrative anchors)
§ 1.4   DELEVERAGINGS
STAGE 04 / 04  ·  THE FORMULA

$$\pi_{\text{total}} \;=\; \Delta M_0^{\%GDP} + \Delta CB^{\%GDP}$$
Regime
If printing dominates the lever mix, deleveraging is beautiful; if austerity + defaults dominate, it is ugly deflationary.
§ 1.5   PARADIGM SHIFTS
STAGE 01 / 04  ·  THE QUESTION

§ 1.5   PARADIGM SHIFTS
STAGE 02 / 04  ·  THE MECHANISM

01
Asset returns invert Best-performing assets of the prior regime become worst-performing.
02
Policy reversal Central bank pivot from easing to tightening (or vice versa).
03
Sentiment flip Market consensus shifts from confidence to fear.
§ 1.5   PARADIGM SHIFTS
STAGE 03 / 04  ·  HISTORY

1970s inflation paradigm. 1980s disinflation paradigm. 2000s quantitative easing paradigm. 2020s likely transition.

§ 1.5   PARADIGM SHIFTS
STAGE 04 / 04  ·  THE FORMULA

$$R_t \;=\; \arg\max_r \; P(r \mid X_t)$$
Regime
Paradigms last 10–20 years; transitions are the source of greatest opportunity and risk.

§ 1.7   INFLATION & CURRENCY
STAGE 02 / 04  ·  THE MECHANISM

01

§ 1.7   INFLATION & CURRENCY

$$\pi_t \;=\; \Delta M_t - \Delta Y_t + \Delta V_t$$
Regime
Currency status determines whether debt resolution is inflationary or deflationary.

§ 2.3   ALPHA & PORTABLE ALPHA
STAGE 01 / 04  ·  THE QUESTION

"alpha is independent from beta and is overlaid on the beta." Dalio · Engineering Targeted Returns and Risks · Bridgewater Aug 2011 · p.8
§ 2.3   ALPHA & PORTABLE ALPHA
STAGE 02 / 04  ·  THE MECHANISM

01
Beta Systematic exposure to asset classes; unlimited capacity.
02
Alpha Skill-based excess return; capacity-constrained.
03
Portable alpha Apply alpha (often via futures/swaps) on top of any beta exposure.
§ 2.3   ALPHA & PORTABLE ALPHA
STAGE 03 / 04  ·  HISTORY

Pure Alpha targets ~12% annualized excess return uncorrelated with beta. Combined with various betas to produce custom return profiles.

§ 2.3   ALPHA & PORTABLE ALPHA
STAGE 04 / 04  ·  THE FORMULA

$$R_p \;=\; \beta + \alpha + \epsilon$$
Regime
Alpha and beta are independent risk premia; portable alpha allows arbitrary combination.
§ 2.4   RISK PARITY & LEVERAGE
STAGE 01 / 04  ·  THE QUESTION

"All Weather doesn't use very much leverage; the strategy is around 2 times leveraged." Dalio / Bridgewater · Engineering Targeted Returns and Risks · p.11
§ 2.4   RISK PARITY & LEVERAGE
STAGE 02 / 04  ·  THE MECHANISM

01
Volatility scaling Compute σ for each asset class.
02
Equal-risk weighting w_i ∝ 1/σ_i.
03
Total leverage Lever the portfolio to target total volatility (e.g. 10%).
§ 2.4   RISK PARITY & LEVERAGE
STAGE 03 / 04  ·  HISTORY

All-Weather is a risk-parity portfolio levered ~2× on bonds. Survived 2008 with shallow drawdown.

§ 2.4   RISK PARITY & LEVERAGE
STAGE 04 / 04  ·  THE FORMULA

$$w_i \;=\; \frac{1/\sigma_i}{\sum_j 1/\sigma_j} \cdot L$$
Regime
Leverage transforms low-vol assets from minor contributors to equal-risk peers.
