§ 1.4  ·  IN CONTEXT

Twelve frameworks, three regimes, four levers.

Dalio's economic engine produces three classical outcomes when a long-term debt cycle tops: ugly deflationary, beautiful, or ugly inflationary. The path that obtains depends on how policymakers mix four levers — debt reduction, austerity, wealth transfer, debt monetisation.

This page renders all twelve frameworks. We start with the deleveragings sub-model — the moment where the regime branches.

§ 1.4   DELEVERAGINGS
STAGE 01 / 04  ·  THE QUESTION

When debt tops out, who bears the loss?

"The differences between deleveragings depend on the amounts and paces of (1) debt reduction, (2) austerity, (3) transferring wealth from the haves to the have-nots, and (4) debt monetisation." Ray Dalio · An In-Depth Look at Deleveragings · 2012
§ 1.4   DELEVERAGINGS
STAGE 02 / 04  ·  THE FOUR LEVERS

Each lever shrinks debt-to-income — but with different side effects.

01
Debt reduction Defaults, restructurings, write-downs. Investors absorb the loss.
Deflationary · Depressing
02
Austerity Cut spending. Households and governments reduce consumption.
Deflationary · Depressing
03
Wealth transfer Tax the haves; redistribute to the have-nots. Dalio: rare in scale.
Neutral · Political
04
Debt monetisation Central bank prints money, buys debt. Currency dilutes.
Inflationary · Stimulative

§ 1.4   DELEVERAGINGS
STAGE 04 / 04  ·  THE FORMULA

The beautiful gate: nominal growth must exceed nominal interest.

$$G_t \;=\; \mathrm{NGDP}_{yoy,t} \;-\; r_t$$

If $G_t > 0$ and $\Delta D_t < 0$, the deleveraging is beautiful. If $G_t < 0$ and $\Delta D_t > 0$, it is ugly deflationary.

U.S. 1930–32 · Worked
$G = -17.0\% - 3.4\% = -20.4\,\mathrm{pp}$
$\Delta D = +97\,\mathrm{pp}$   (DebtGDP $155\% \to 252\%$)
$\pi = 0.4\% + 0.4\% = 0.8\%$ — print intensity stayed low
Regime
G < 0  and  ΔD > 0  ⇒  ugly deflationary.
